A Guide for Florida Parents

Getting Your Child Into a Top Florida Private School

A complete, no-nonsense timeline for private, independent, and boarding school admissions in Florida, from your first tour to enrollment day.

Florida is home to one of the deepest independent school landscapes in the country, and one of the most competitive. Schools like Pine Crest, Ransom Everglades, and American Heritage routinely receive far more applications than they have seats, and the families who get in are rarely the ones who started looking a few weeks before the deadline.

This guide lays out exactly what to expect and when, drawing on published guidance from the National Association of Independent Schools (NAIS) and the organizations that administer private school admissions testing, alongside what we see directly with South Florida families.

Step 1: Map Your Timeline 12–18 Months Out

Florida independent schools admit new students mainly at three points: kindergarten or early childhood, a middle-school entry year (commonly 6th grade), and 9th grade. Applications for each typically open in the fall of the prior school year. Starting your search this early gives you time to tour multiple campuses, test more than once if needed, and apply to a realistic range of schools rather than a single reach.

Step 2: Understand ISEE vs. SSAT Testing

Most Florida day schools for grades 5 and up require the ISEE, administered by the Educational Records Bureau (ERB). Boarding schools and some secondary-entry programs more often use the SSAT, administered by the Enrollment Management Association. Confirm which exam each school on your list accepts before registering, since not all schools accept both.

Step 3: Build an Application That Tells a Story

Beyond test scores, admissions committees weigh transcripts, teacher recommendations, a parent statement, and, for older applicants, a short writing sample. The strongest applications present a coherent picture of who a student is and what kind of school community they will contribute to, not just a list of achievements.

Step 4: Prepare for Both the Student and Parent Interview

Nearly every Florida independent school includes an interview component, both for the applicant and for parents. These conversations are assessing fit and alignment with the school's mission, not administering a pass/fail test. Families who can speak specifically to why a particular school, not just "private school" in general, is right for their child stand out.

Step 5: Don't Rule Out a School on Tuition Alone

Most Florida independent schools maintain need-based financial aid programs, and NAIS reports member schools collectively award well over a billion dollars in aid each year. Financial aid decisions are typically made separately from, and sometimes ahead of, the admissions decision. Apply for aid at the same time you apply for admission.

Step 6: Compare Day, Boarding, and Public Magnet Options

Our Our Schools page organizes South Florida's options into three groups worth comparing honestly before you commit to a search strategy:

  • Private day schools: Pine Crest, Gulliver Preparatory, Ransom Everglades, American Heritage, The Benjamin School, Oxbridge Academy, The King's Academy, Jupiter Christian, Carrollton School of the Sacred Heart, Miami Country Day, Palmer Trinity, and Westminster Christian.
  • Boarding schools: Saint Andrew's School, Admiral Farragut Academy, and Florida Air Academy, for families considering a residential program.
  • Public & magnet schools: MAST Academy, Design and Architecture Senior High (DASH), Coral Reef Senior High School, and Suncoast Community High School, offering selective, tuition-free specialized tracks.

FAQs

Common questions from Florida parents

When should I start applying to private school in Florida?

Ideally 12 to 18 months before your target entry year. Most Florida independent schools open applications in the fall for the following August, and the earlier you begin touring and testing, the more options and financial aid opportunities remain open.

What test does my child need to take?

Most Florida day schools for grades 5 and above require the ISEE, administered by the Educational Records Bureau (ERB). Some schools, particularly boarding schools, accept or require the SSAT instead. Always confirm which exam a specific school accepts before registering.

Is financial aid available at private schools in Florida?

Yes. Most independent schools in Florida offer need-based financial aid, and NAIS member schools collectively award well over a billion dollars annually. Aid is typically assessed separately from the admissions decision, so sticker price alone should not rule out a school.

What's the difference between a boarding school and a day school?

Day schools have students commute from home each day, while boarding schools provide on-campus residential housing. A small number of Florida schools, such as Saint Andrew's School, Admiral Farragut Academy, and Florida Air Academy, offer boarding options alongside day enrollment.

Are public magnet schools a good alternative?

For the right student, yes. Schools such as MAST Academy and Design and Architecture Senior High (DASH) offer highly specialized, selective public programs at no tuition cost, though they have their own competitive application process and limited seats.
